# Multi -Drug Resistance - Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR)

**Paklihawa High School, Siddharthanagar–01, Rupandehi**

On 19th March, an Antimicrobial Resistance (AMR) Awareness Program was successfully conducted at Paklihawa High School, Siddharthanagar–01, Rupandehi, under the supervision of Youth Activism Nepal. The session brought together a diverse group of secondary-level students and educators with the aim of strengthening awareness on one of the most pressing global public health challenges—antibiotic resistance.

Antimicrobial Resistance is increasingly recognized as a silent pandemic. According to global health estimates, AMR is directly responsible for approximately 1.27 million deaths annually and contributes to nearly 5 million deaths worldwide each year. In South Asia, including Nepal, the burden is particularly concerning due to factors such as over-the-counter access to antibiotics, lack of awareness, and inconsistent prescription practices. Studies conducted in Nepal have shown rising resistance patterns among common bacterial pathogens, especially in infections caused by Gram-negative bacteria, highlighting the urgent need for awareness at the community level.

The session at Paklihawa High School was designed to address this knowledge gap by introducing students to the basic science of antibiotics, the causes and consequences of misuse, and the long-term risks associated with resistance. Using interactive discussions, real-life examples, and practical scenarios, the facilitators emphasized how behaviors such as incomplete antibiotic courses, self-medication, and misuse in livestock can accelerate the development of resistant bacteria.

A key focus of the program was the One Health approach, which recognizes the interconnectedness of human health, animal health, and the environment. Students were introduced to the concept that antibiotic misuse in agriculture and veterinary practices can contribute to resistance that ultimately affects human populations. By illustrating local examples and global case studies, the session encouraged participants to think beyond individual health choices and consider broader ecological impacts.

The program also incorporated short assessments and engagement activities to evaluate understanding and encourage participation. Feedback collected at the end of the session indicated a significant improvement in students’ awareness levels. Many participants reported that it was their first time learning about AMR in a structured way, and they expressed a strong interest in sharing this knowledge within their families and communities.

This initiative was carried out under Project BioLiberty, a flagship effort of Youth Activism Nepal aimed at promoting scientific awareness and responsible health practices among young people. The program is supported by the Global Accelerator Program (GAP) from Students For Liberty, which provides a platform for youth-led initiatives addressing critical global challenges.

The successful implementation of this session was made possible through the active efforts of team members Sudarshan Shrestha, Kritak Parajuli, Aayush Tharu, and Nirmal Puri. Their coordination, facilitation, and commitment ensured that the program was both informative and engaging. The initiative was conducted under the leadership of President Rishav  Das, whose continued efforts in youth mobilization and public health advocacy have been central to expanding the reach of such programs across Nepal.

The Paklihawa session represents a step forward in building a generation that is informed, responsible, and proactive in addressing AMR. Youth Activism Nepal remains committed to scaling such interventions in schools and communities, with the long-term goal of fostering behavioral change and strengthening public health awareness nationwide.
