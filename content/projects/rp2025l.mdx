# Repurpose-Lamjung

**Practical Workshop At Shree Bhakti Namuna Secondary School,**

At Shree Bhakti Namuna Secondary School in Lamjung, Project Repurpose was implemented as a combined awareness and practical learning initiative, engaging students in understanding the link between everyday habits and broader environmental challenges such as climate change. The program emphasized that individual actions particularly consumption patterns and waste disposal practices play a significant role in shaping environmental outcomes at the local and global levels.

The session began with a facilitated discussion on climate change, focusing on how plastic consumption, improper waste management, and increasing dependency on single-use materials contribute to environmental degradation. Students were introduced to key concepts such as carbon footprint, resource efficiency, and the long-term impact of non-biodegradable waste on ecosystems. In Nepal, where solid waste management systems are still developing, plastic waste remains a persistent challenge, often ending up in open dumps or natural landscapes, contributing indirectly to greenhouse gas emissions and environmental pollution.

Following the awareness segment, the program transitioned into a practical session where students actively engaged in creating do-it-yourself (DIY) products using repurposed materials. Plastic bottles, snack wrappers, and other commonly discarded items were transformed into useful household products, decorative items, and simple storage solutions. This hands-on approach allowed students to directly apply the concepts discussed, reinforcing the idea that waste can be managed creatively and responsibly.

The practical component also encouraged teamwork, problem-solving, and innovation, as students worked collaboratively to design and present their repurposed products. Facilitators guided the process, ensuring that students understood not only the “how” but also the “why” behind repurposing linking these small actions to larger environmental benefits such as waste reduction and resource conservation.

One of the most notable outcomes of the session has been its continuation beyond the workshop itself. The repurposed products created by students have inspired a schoolwide initiative, with the administration and student body planning a competition aimed at reducing plastic consumption. This student-led effort reflects a growing sense of ownership and responsibility, demonstrating how awareness programs can translate into sustained action within the school community.

The initiative aligns with the broader goal of fostering environmentally conscious youth who are capable of leading change within their local contexts. In a district like Lamjung, where natural resources and landscapes are integral to community life, such efforts contribute to building a culture of sustainability at the grassroots level.

The session at Shree Bhakti Namuna Secondary School stands as a strong example of how combining awareness with practical engagement can create lasting impact. By connecting personal habits to climate change and empowering students with actionable solutions, Project Repurpose continues to support the development of student-led initiatives toward a greener and more sustainable Lamjung.
