# Repurpose-Pokahara

**At Janapriya Secondary School, Simalchor,Pokara **

An awareness session under Project Repurpose was conducted at Janapriya Secondary School, Simalchor, Pokhara, with the objective of introducing students to the growing challenge of plastic waste and the importance of responsible waste management. The session was designed as a facilitation-based learning experience, focusing on knowledge sharing, discussion, and awareness rather than hands-on activities.

Pokhara, as one of Nepal’s प्रमुख tourism hubs, faces increasing pressure from urbanization and waste generation. Municipal estimates suggest that a significant portion of urban solid waste consists of plastic materials, including bottles, wrappers, and packaging. When not properly managed, this waste often finds its way into drainage systems and nearby water bodies, posing risks to ecosystems such as Phewa Lake and affecting the city’s environmental and tourism value.

During the session, students were introduced to the concept of plastic waste management, with a particular emphasis on reducing dependency on single-use plastics and understanding their long-term environmental impact. Facilitators explained how commonly used items like plastic bottles and snack wrappers contribute to pollution when discarded irresponsibly. The discussion extended to the broader implications of plastic waste, including its role in clogging waterways, harming aquatic life, and gradually breaking down into microplastics that can enter the food chain.

The session also highlighted the idea of repurposing as an alternative approach within the waste management hierarchy. Rather than demonstrating practical activities, facilitators guided students through examples and concepts of how waste materials can be given a second life through creative reuse. This approach aimed to shift perception—from viewing plastic as disposable to recognizing its potential utility when managed responsibly.

A key focus of the program was aligning local actions with broader environmental goals. Students were encouraged to understand their role in maintaining Pokhara’s identity as a clean and sustainable city. As a destination known for its natural beauty and lakes, even small behavioral changes at the individual level can contribute to preserving the environment and supporting the local economy dependent on tourism.

The session maintained an interactive format, allowing students to ask questions, share observations from their own surroundings, and reflect on their daily consumption habits. This dialogue-driven approach helped make the issue more relatable and emphasized personal responsibility in addressing environmental challenges.

The initiative reflects Youth Activism Nepal’s commitment to promoting awareness-driven change through education and engagement. By focusing on facilitation and informed discussion, Project Repurpose aims to build a foundation of environmental consciousness among young individuals, encouraging them to adopt sustainable practices in their everyday lives.

The session at Janapriya Secondary School represents a step toward fostering informed youth who are aware of the environmental impact of plastic waste and are motivated to contribute to cleaner, more sustainable communities.
